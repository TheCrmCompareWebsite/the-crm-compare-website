How to choose a real estate CRM

Start with who will use it. A solo agent, an agent with an assistant, a high-volume team and a brokerage need different things. Each of our reports names the fit.

Price the plan you'll actually use. We score the typical plan — the one most agents buy — and list add-ons like calling or texting separately.

Read the contract. Free trials, cancellation, refunds and price increases vary widely: terms-of-service scores across the 26 CRMs we scored run from 1.0 to 10.0.

Check who owns your data. Our governance score looks at the company and its privacy policy, including whether client data can be sold or reused.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a real estate CRM cost?

Across the 26 CRMs we scored, the typical plan costs from $179 a year (RealtyJuggler) to $11,988 a year (CINC). The median is $1,044 a year, and 12 of the 26 cost less than $1,000 a year.

Should I use a real estate CRM or a general CRM?

Real estate CRMs come with lead routing, transaction tracking and past-client tools built in. The 7 general CRMs we scored (such as Apptivo, CloseCRM and Pipedrive) are sales tools you'd have to set up yourself; the best of them, Apptivo, scored 7.7.

Which CRM has the most features?

RealtyJuggler earned the most points on our checklist: 103.5 of 119. More features isn't always better — see each report's value score, which weighs features against price.

How do you score each CRM?

Each CRM gets a score out of 10 in six categories: features, reviews, value, terms of service, support and governance. The final score is the average of the six. Read the full methodology.

Which CRM is best for a team?

For high-volume teams, Follow Up Boss scored highest among CRMs built mainly for teams (6.5). For an agent with an assistant, look at Realvolve (6.3).

Introduction

RealtyJuggler is a CRM built specifically for real estate agents. RealOrganized, Inc., its maker, markets it to new agents, individual experienced agents, and teams. It can also be used by a broker for an entire office of agents.

RealOrganized is a small, privately held company based in Fort Collins, Colorado. It began in 2003, when software engineer Scott Schmitz, who had worked at Apple and AOL, joined forces with Davette Everly, a top-producing agent near Washington, D.C., who wanted simpler agent software. The product went on sale the following year. More than two decades later, Schmitz remains president and directs development, working with a small staff, most of whom have been with the organization for over 10 years. In September 2026, he published “Real Estate CRM Secrets,” a book that shows first-year agents how to build a business around a CRM. A sequel, “Real Estate CRM Mastery,” is scheduled for release in the first quarter of 2027 and is geared toward more experienced agents. Both books aim to explain how a real estate CRM can boost business and make an agent more productive. Most agents use only a fraction of the functionality available in their CRM.

RealtyJuggler COM focuses on an easy-to-use product, with personalized support and training, all at an affordable price. The product is offered at a single pricing tier that includes onboarding, coaching, and live phone support.

Fit

RealtyJuggler is best suited for new agents, individual agents working independently, agents with an assistant, and real estate offices.

A new agent gets an affordable system to carry them through a lean first year, a long free trial to learn it, and a real person to call for setup, coaching, and help along the way. The tools for meeting new people and following up with them are ready to use, along with a large stock of ready-made content, including eCards, Letters, and flyers, so there is little to build before getting to work.

An experienced agent working alone gets nearly everything needed to run a business built on past clients and referrals while still gathering and incubating new leads. Leads from websites and portals arrive without retyping, and the system maintains a steady rhythm of staying in touch with a sphere of influence on schedule. The affordable price makes this CRM a great fit for a part-time or semi-retired agent. Don’t be fooled by the low price into thinking the product lacks features. Its feature set is quite competitive with CRMs costing many times its price.

An agent can share their database with an assistant or an entire team. Features include assigning tasks, tracking progress, and delegating routine work such as mailings, follow-ups, and keeping deals on track. A change log and the option to restore deleted or altered records help protect the agent's business while the assistant works in the database.

A broker can use RealtyJuggler to let each agent track and incubate their own leads while the broker supervises the entire office. Each agent sees only the leads assigned to them. The long free trial suits brokers who may experience high agent turnover. The broker pays for seats only after the 90-day trial ends, so the office doesn't pay for agents who leave soon after arriving. Training and phone support come with every membership, easing the ongoing work of bringing new agents up to speed.

One issue reviewers have noted is the simple user interface, which can look dated. A second limitation is the 10,000-record limit per agent, which makes RealtyJuggler a poor fit for a high-volume team. However, for agents for whom these two limitations are not a factor, RealtyJuggler is well worth considering.

Features 8.7/10

RealtyJuggler includes nearly all standard real estate features in its single plan.

RealtyJuggler offers a well-balanced set of features, including a fully featured transaction manager, client service reports, robust print and email marketing, and financial tools for tracking income, expenses, goals, and projected closings. Team controls are unusually detailed for the price, and leads from portals and websites flow in without re-entry, thanks to both email parsing and customized CSV importers. The weaknesses lie in automation and in limits on the total number of records the CRM can handle. These matter little to a solo agent working a sphere of influence or even a small team. For a high-volume team, these limitations will make the CRM less appealing.

Several features receive half credit because they are incomplete. Every lead that arrives through the Email Feed automatically receives a call task. However, drip sequences and task plans must be started manually. Leads are not automatically assigned to team members, so for broker configurations, the broker must assign leads to individual agents by transferring them rather than through automation. Email open tracking can determine whether someone opened an email and when, but not which links were clicked. RealtyJuggler does not offer an integrated IDX website, but can connect with third-party websites through email lead parsing.

An assistant can be restricted to read-only or no-delete access and excluded from income and expense records. Tasks can be assigned to team members using categories. A client or loan officer can receive a read-only web link to a deal's summary but cannot edit it or add notes. An office shares mail forms by sending copies rather than through a single shared library. While the CRM offers individualized reporting, there are no reporting features that compare or rank team members' activity.

Two features outside the standard list stand out. The continuing-education module tracks the continuing education classes an agent needs to take to renew their license, and the rental module tracks tenants, rents, expenses, and work orders for agents who also manage rental properties.

There is no integrated AI built into RealtyJuggler. The CRM, however, integrates with AI chatbots via skills and synchronizes contacts and calendars using the built-in Google sync capability.

Some limits are deliberate. Bulk texting is excluded because it requires each recipient's written permission, and agents who want it are directed to third-party services. There is no app-store app; the mobile version is a web app you can install on the home screen. Synchronization runs through Google, which also covers Apple devices, but not Outlook. No newsletter is included. Each account holds up to 5,000 contacts plus 5,000 prospects, for a total of 10,000 records.

Terms of Service 10/10

RealtyJuggler's written terms favor the customer at nearly every point. The pricing page offers a 90-day free trial with no credit card required, and the trial does not automatically convert to a paid membership. Under the Terms & Conditions, "The free trial will expire after 90 days," and it becomes a membership only when the member clicks Buy Now.

The only option is a 12-month membership, billed annually, but paying for a full year doesn’t lock you in. Either party can cancel at any time. The Terms promise a prorated refund for the unused membership upon request, and, as noted on the security page, memberships don’t renew automatically. If a membership isn’t paid, it expires but remains readable for at least 60 days. Existing members are also protected from price increases: their rate will never exceed the amount they first paid. New members have paid $179 since January 2019.

The terms also strike a balance between the two sides. The company may cancel a membership at any time with written notice, and the member has the same right. Either way, the member receives a prorated refund, so the company cannot close an account and keep the unused fee. Two clauses favor the company, as they do in most software agreements: it may change the Terms by posting a new version, with continued use counting as acceptance; and any claim must be brought within one year, while the company's liability is capped at the amount the member paid.

Disputes are governed by Colorado law and are heard in Larimer County courts. Arbitration through the BBB applies only if the member agrees to it in writing.

Support 10/10

With RealtyJuggler, phone support, onboarding, training, and data import are included with the membership.

Phone technical support is available from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Mountain Time on weekdays, with callbacks for voicemail. It is open to free-trial users as well as paying members. The number appears in the header of the home page. According to the About page, staff at the company's office in the USA handle all technical support, and reviewers consistently say they reach someone who can fix the problem.

Onboarding is free and goes beyond a welcome call. The support page lists setting up the member's letterhead and portrait, importing contacts and prospects, syncing smartphones, and customized one-on-one training for the member and an assistant. If an existing database is in an unusual format, staff will build a custom importer for it at no charge, as the FAQ explains.

For self-help, the manuals and tutorials page offers a main manual of more than 14,000 words, six topic manuals, a comprehensive FAQ, and about 72 captioned video tutorials in both Spanish and English. Email deliverability receives specific attention: one staff member's role includes helping members with it, and two tutorials cover the subject.

Company 9.5/10

RealOrganized, Inc. is a small, privately owned software company with a long record and a clear public profile.

The company was founded in 2003 and released RealtyJuggler in September 2004. It is based at 149 W Harvard Street in Fort Collins, Colorado, an address that matches its BBB profile and Google Business Profile. The About page lists the founders and staff: co-founder Scott Schmitz is president and still leads engineering. The core team has been in place for over 10 years, with one addition in 2025. LinkedIn lists 11 to 50 employees, and Crunchbase shows no outside funding.

RealOrganized sells only RealtyJuggler, and none of its staff are real estate agents. Two ties to the broader CRM market are worth noting. In September 2026, Schmitz published “Real Estate CRM Secrets,” a book for new agents that the company's announcement presents as a companion to RealtyJuggler. The About page also states that a sequel for experienced agents, “Real Estate CRM Mastery,” will be available in 2027. The company has conflicts of interest and sells only RealtyJuggler.

The BBB rates the company A+, and it has been an accredited business since December 2006, with no complaints on file. Privacy Policy 10/10

RealtyJuggler's privacy policy treats an agent's contacts as private information, prohibits selling or reusing them, and allows the agent to export the data at any time.

The Privacy Policy states plainly, "We will not sell or rent Member Private Information or Member Account Information," and clarifies that the vendor acts only as a processor of that information, using it to provide the service. AI features are similarly constrained: "Insights derived from one Member's data are never used to train, inform, or improve outputs" for other members, and nothing is pooled across accounts.

The protections are built to last. Any change that expands the use or sharing of a member's data, or weakens a protection, requires that member's opt-in consent. The policy would bind a buyer of the company, a commitment the Terms & Conditions repeat.

After a membership ends, the account remains readable for at least 60 days, and its contacts and prospects can still be exported as CSV files. On request, the vendor permanently deletes the data. RealtyJuggler does not build or host agents' websites; leads that arrive from an agent's own site become part of the agent's contacts and fall under the same policy.

The Privacy Policy's section on changes contains two versions of the same paragraph. The first requires a member's opt-in consent only before broader use applies to data already collected, leaving room to apply new terms to data added later; the second, fuller version requires consent whether the data was collected before or after the change and also covers changes that weaken a protection. The fuller version is clearly the intended rule, and the company should remove the older paragraph so that members know which one governs.

The policy's list of sharing purposes opens by stating that both account information and members' private information are used for every purpose on the list, including sales support and announcements of new features and services. The Data Controllership section that follows limits the use of members' private information to providing the service, keeping it secure, and meeting legal requirements, and assigns the other purposes to account information only. The later section states the intended rule, but the opening sentence of the list should be narrowed to match it.

  • +1It cannot be changed to be less favorable without opt-in consent
  • +1A new owner is bound by it
  • +1The vendor handles end-customer data only as a processor

7 + 6 = 13, above the top of the scale, so 10.

Conclusion

RealtyJuggler is a CRM built exclusively for real estate and is a good fit for new agents, individual agents, and agents with an assistant. A real estate office can run every agent on it. High-volume teams and franchises that need site licensing may be tempted, but should probably look elsewhere.

RealtyJuggler’s strengths include a broad set of useful tools in a single plan, terms that let an agent try it at length and leave at any time, a privacy policy that keeps clients' information private and portable, and personal help from setup through daily use, as reviewers on every site confirm. Its weaknesses include a dated look, limited automatic handling of incoming leads, and a database size ceiling that most agents are unlikely to notice.

No critical red flags were identified. The company is long established, still run by its founder, and has no mortgage, title, or brokerage business that could compete for an agent's clients.

With setup, training, and support included in a low annual fee, RealtyJuggler is an excellent value for the agents it is built for, and an office pays the same low rate for each agent.

Alternatives

  • Wise Agent: A real estate CRM run by its founders since 2002, priced at $499 per year, with round-the-clock phone, chat, and email support, plus free onboarding and training. It most closely matches RealtyJuggler in approach and service and is built for high-volume teams, with paid add-ons for texting, dialing, and AI at nearly three times the base price.
  • IXACT Contact: A sphere-focused real estate CRM from Elm Street Technology, priced at $561 per year, best suited for average agents and those with an assistant. It offers ready-made marketing campaigns and monthly billing, but it costs about three times as much, and its reviews fell sharply from 2023 to 2025 before recovering in 2026.
  • Follow Up Boss: Zillow's lead-focused CRM, priced at $696 per year on its Grow plan, built for high-volume teams and agents with an assistant. It is the stronger choice for teams that route purchased leads, where RealtyJuggler falls short, but its governance score is low because of how Zillow's terms handle client data.
  • Referral Maker CRM: Buffini & Company's referral-focused CRM, priced at $468 per year, is built around Buffini's coaching system for agents who work their sphere. It appeals most to agents already in Buffini coaching, though its feature score is far lower, and the company has announced a move to a new platform with BoldTrail.
  • Realvolve: A workflow-centered CRM from Colorado, priced at $790 per year on its Professional plan, built for agents with an assistant and high-volume teams. It offers more automation and a built-in AI assistant than RealtyJuggler, but at more than four times the price and with no phone support.
  • Grove: A new, sphere-focused CRM launched in November 2026 by Elm Street Technology, the owner of IXACT Contact, at $600 per year. It is simple and aimed at the average solo agent, but its feature set is limited, and it has no customer reviews or track record yet.
  • Top Producer: One of the oldest real estate CRMs, now owned by Constellation, costs $2,148 per year on its Pro plan and is built for experienced solo agents, agents with an assistant, and high-volume teams. It includes branded market reports and listing alerts, but it requires a 12-month contract, offers no free trial or phone support, and costs about twelve times as much.
  • BoldTrail: On Inside Real Estate's platform, formerly kvCORE, it is sold primarily to brokerages and franchises at about $5,988 per year for a single seat. It serves large offices and franchises that RealtyJuggler does not, but its reviews and contract terms score far lower, and it costs more than thirty times as much.
  • Market Leader: A lead-generation CRM owned by Constellation Software, the owner of Top Producer, priced at $2,268 per year on its Professional plan and built for agents with an assistant and high-volume teams. It offers the purchased-lead flow RealtyJuggler lacks, but its reviews and contract terms score far lower, and it costs more than twelve times as much.

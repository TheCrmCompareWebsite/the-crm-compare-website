Introduction

Insightly is a customer relationship management system for business-to-business sales. It is not designed specifically for real estate agents. Its maker pitches it to small and mid-sized companies in fields such as professional services, manufacturing, and financial services, particularly to firms whose relationship with a customer continues after the sale while the work is delivered. Real estate agents, teams, and brokerages are not among the buyers it targets.

The company was founded in Perth, Australia, in 2009 by Anthony Smith and has been based in San Francisco since 2012. In July 2024, it became part of Unbounce, a Vancouver-based landing-page software maker owned by the private equity firm Crest Rock Partners. Unbounce's chief executive, Steve Oriola, now oversees both businesses.

It promises a single system that a growing company can use to manage prospects, close sales, and organize the work that follows, tailored to its own process rather than a fixed industry template, with per-user pricing aimed at smaller firms that have outgrown spreadsheets yet don't want a complex and expensive enterprise system.

Fit

Insightly isn't a good choice for real estate agents, teams, or brokerages because it was built for businesses in general and lacks built-in support for how agents find, win, keep, and serve clients. A company prepared to invest in customization could shape it into a workable real estate system by adding its own follow-up routines for buyers, listings, and closings, but that takes time and likely paid help. Some everyday parts of an agent's work, such as texting clients and taking in leads from real estate websites, would still be missing. There is no real estate-specific content library, such as flyers, eCards, and letters. Because the product focuses on selling to businesses, Insightly doesn't handle real estate-specific tasks like tracking open houses, listings, showings, and commission calculations. Real estate CRMs provide these things ready to use, so agents and offices of every size will get further, sooner, with one of them.

Features 4.9/10

Insightly's features score is respectable for a CRM not designed for real estate.

Its strength is the core of a sales system. Contact and company records are detailed and linked to one another and to deals, with roles and pipelines. Pipelines are flexible, and reporting is unusually deep, with standard dashboards for pipeline value, rep rankings, and lead sources, plus custom reports that compare periods. Two-way email and calendar sync, a mobile app, web forms that create leads, and a full set of team controls, from shared records to role hierarchies, permission profiles, and automatic lead assignment, round it out.

The gaps are in all real estate-specific features. There is no texting, no printing of letters, labels, or envelopes, no connection to listing data, no lockbox, showing or offer tracking, and no agent calculators. Lead intake from real estate portals and IDX websites is missing, and the content an agent would send to clients, such as letters, drip campaigns, flyers, and cards, is not available in the product.

Email marketing is a separate product. Drip campaigns, newsletters, bounce and opt-out handling, and deliverability reporting all belong to Insightly Marketing, sold separately for $99 a month. This is why several email rows receive only half credit. The CRM itself can send individual and bulk emails from templates, but not timed sequences.

Many real estate needs can be met with Insightly's customization tools, as described in the help center. A business can add custom objects for listings and offers, date fields for contract deadlines, and workflows or activity sets for follow-up. Deals already include close dates, linked parties with roles, and file storage, which is why transaction management and key dates earn partial credit. The same applies to bringing in leads from a particular source: every spreadsheet import must be mapped to Insightly's fields manually, and a reusable importer can be built only as a recipe in AppConnect, Insightly's separately sold integration builder. Anything an agent has to build this way earns no credit here, and Insightly provides no real estate templates to start from.

Some useful features sit above the Professional plan or come with conditions. A full audit log of who changed which field is limited to Enterprise. Contact sync with Google runs only one way, from Insightly to Google. Outside vendors such as a transaction coordinator need their own paid user seat, because there is no guest access.

Insightly is rapidly adding AI and developer tools. The Copilot assistant answers questions about records and drafts content. An MCP server, added in October 2026, lets external AI tools read and update CRM data. A documented REST API is available on every plan, and webhooks come with Professional. Professional also allows 250,000 records, far more than most agents will ever need.

Price

Insightly offers three CRM plans, all priced per user per month and quoted for annual billing. The Professional plan, which Insightly labels "Recommended," is the typical plan, at $588 per year for one user ($49 per month billed annually). A first year with the onboarding and training packages Insightly sells totals $5,088.

Positive

Ease of use is the most consistent strength, praised in more than 100 of the reviews studied. New users report getting productive quickly: "Within the first week of use, I felt more comfortable than I did with our prior CRM" (G2, Aug 2025), and a management consultant wrote that "The best part about Insightly is how user friendly it is" (TrustRadius, Aug 2025). Reviewers also appreciate how far the system can be tailored to their sales process, and that the work sold in a deal can be tracked through delivery in the same place. A regional business development manager summed up the appeal: "It's flexible. You can set up multiple sales pipelines and stages" (G2, Apr 2026).

Connections to the other business tools customers already use also draw praise, though nearly as many reviewers found setup harder than expected.

Negative

Support is the dominant complaint. More than forty reviews, many from customers with five or ten years of experience, describe slow, unhelpful, or missing support, compared with about twenty that praise it. "“<24-hour” support consistently turns into days—or even weeks—before anyone replies," wrote the CEO of a ten-year customer (Trustpilot, Jul 2025). A 2026 buyer reported that "you create a ticket.. and no replies" (Trustpilot, Feb 2026); another described automated video replies followed by "about 24 hours to get a response, and it still didn't resolve our issue" (G2, Mar 2026); and a longtime customer wrote that "Support often takes days to respond" (Google Business Profile, 2026). The same Trustpilot buyer said sales pushed him into a year's prepayment, and older Google Business Profile reviews make the same overpromising charge. Insightly answers some critical G2 reviews with an apology and an offer to help, but none of the replies we read shows the problem was solved.

Billing and cancellation problems recur. A 2026 BBB complaint, still unanswered, states that a customer who had paid by wire continued to receive overdue and suspension notices and that "We have received no acknowledgment and no human reply whatsoever." A Google Business Profile reviewer was locked out after a card error at renewal, and older reviews describe charges after canceling and an auto-renewal that was hard to stop.

Unfixed bugs are the third theme, and the mobile app divides opinion. Reviewers report automations that fail to trigger, notifications that stop after updates, and frequent timeouts. Some users rely on the app every day, but the June 2026 rebuild brought crashes, a broken date picker, and a broken Google sign-in. "This app is painful and time-consuming to use," an iPhone user wrote (App Store, Aug 2026); another iPhone user reported that support fixed the sign-in problem in a later release. Reporting divides opinion: some find reports easy to build, while others run into limits on what a report can show. "Reporting and analytics were probably my number one complaint" (G2, Apr 2026).

Value 6.6/10

Insightly's typical plan, Professional, costs $588 per year, and its normalized feature score is 4.9, so each feature point costs about $120. Neither number stands out on its own: the price is moderate for a per-user CRM, and the feature set covers about half of what agents look for. Together, they make a fair buy on paper.

Terms of Service 9/10

Insightly's pricing is published and simple, with three plans. The 14-day free trial runs at the recommended Professional level without a credit card, so a trial cannot turn into a charge by accident. Customers can pay month to month or choose annual billing for a discount, according to Insightly's help article on managing a subscription.

The commitments that follow a purchase are more binding than the trial suggests. Under the Terms of Service, a subscription renews automatically for the same term unless the customer gives at least 30 days' notice before it ends, and renewals are charged at the then-current list price. Reviewers on Google Business Profile, Capterra, and the App Store report charges after canceling and accounts they could not close. Insightly's help article warns that its self-service cancel button closes the account permanently, so a customer who only wants to stop billing has to contact Insightly instead. Disputes are subject to arbitration in San Francisco under Delaware law.

The contract leans toward Insightly on money. A customer who stops before the end of a term still owes the remaining fees and receives no refund. The same bill comes due if Insightly ends the agreement due to the customer's breach. Insightly can also end it immediately, without notice, if the customer violates its Privacy Policy. Money flows back only if Insightly itself commits a breach and fails to fix it. Neither side can walk away mid-term without cause. On monthly billing, the obligation is small, but a customer who chose annual billing for its discount is committed for the full year.

Recent changes have cost current users some functionality. Insightly's own article about its mobile app says the June 2026 rebuild omitted six features from the previous app that customers had been using. One Trustpilot reviewer in 2026 said the price "doubled" at renewal, but we found no other recent source confirming a price increase.

  • +1The free trial
  • +1The trial requires no credit card
  • +0.5The shortest plan term is monthly
  • −0.5The June 2026 mobile app rebuild removed features customers used

7 + 2.5 − 0.5 = 9.

Support 9/10

Insightly's support score reflects what it offers on paper. Phone and email support are included with every plan. Insightly's pricing page states that all users "can reach our support team by phone or email," and its help article on contacting support explains that the phone line is accessed from inside the app. No support number appears on the website's contact or home pages.

The self-help material is extensive. The help center was rebuilt in mid-2026 and contains about 200 articles. The Insightly CRM YouTube channel has more than 450 videos, and a webinar library is linked from the help center.

Hands-on help costs extra. Guided Onboarding, four to six Zoom sessions with an onboarding specialist covering configuration, data imports, reports, and admin training, is sold separately. The Premier Support and Success plan, priced as a share of license fees, adds individualized training, a customer success manager, a priority queue, and longer phone hours (6 to 6 Pacific time on weekdays, instead of 9 to 4). Data migrations and custom integrations are handled by Professional Services under a statement of work and billed on a time-and-materials basis; none of the help Insightly describes, paid or free, includes building reusable importers for a particular lead source's files. Nothing in Insightly's written policy limits support during the 14-day trial.

The support score sits far above the review score of 2, and the gap is the key thing a buyer should understand about Insightly. The written policy is generous, yet the most consistent complaint across Trustpilot, Google Business Profile, TrustRadius, and the app stores is slow or missing support, with replies taking days and tickets going unanswered. Customers on the Premier plan with a named success manager tend to describe a very different experience from those who rely on the standard queue.

How the score was calculated Starts at 7, then:

  • +1Phone support is included with all plans
  • +1.5Onboarding, 1-on-1 training, and help importing data, all available for an extra charge (0.5 each)
  • −0.5The phone number is not listed on the contact or home page

7 + 2.5 − 0.5 = 9.

Governance 7/10

Insightly's governance score is the average of its company score and its privacy policy score.

Company 4.5/10

Insightly, Inc. is an established general-purpose CRM provider that has been part of a private-equity-backed marketing software company since 2024.

Anthony Smith founded Insightly in Perth, Australia, in 2009 and moved it to San Francisco in 2012 (Wikipedia). In July 2024, it merged into Unbounce, the Vancouver landing-page company owned by Denver private equity firm Crest Rock Partners (Crest Rock Partners). Unbounce's CEO, Steve Oriola, now also leads Insightly, and Mike Cravens is VP of Product (Insightly webinar, Aug 2025). Unbounce does not sell another CRM, and we found no ties to real estate brokerage, mortgage, or title businesses.

The product is still being developed. Since the merger, Insightly has shipped a steady stream of AI, email, and integration updates, most recently in October 2026. It rebuilt its mobile app in June 2026 and rewrote its help center in mid-2026. At the same time, support and billing addresses moved to Unbounce domains, and reviewers describe a decline in service after the change in ownership. Employees see the strain too: Insightly is rated 3.4 out of 5 from 100 reviews on Glassdoor, and the three most recent, all from 2026, describe frequent shifts in direction and a weaker culture since the merger.

The website lacks accountability details. The About page states that Insightly is "now an Unbounce company" but names no one in management. The contact page lists no phone number or address; addresses appear only in the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, and its Google Business Profile listing uses a private mailbox in San Francisco. The company holds a C+ rating from the BBB, which notes an unanswered complaint, and is not accredited. Its domain dates from 2008.

  • −0.5The address appears only in the legal documents

  • −1Insightly sells a generic CRM
  • −1The website names no one in management

7 + 2 − 4.5 = 4.5.

Privacy Policy 9.5/10

Insightly's contract treats the contacts and records an agent stores as the customer's own, to be used only to run the service and deleted once the account is canceled.

The Terms of Service state that the customer owns its CRM data and that Insightly "will not access your CRM Data for any purpose other than in connection with the provision of Services." The Privacy Policy does not address that data at all; it describes Insightly as processing information under its clients' direction, with use limited to providing the service the client engaged it for.

The AI tools are covered as well. The Privacy Policy states that data obtained through Insightly's connection to outside AI assistants is not used to train AI models, and that customer data is not used to train publicly available models. When an agent connects an outside AI assistant, the data it retrieves is governed by that assistant's terms, so agents should review those terms before connecting.

Deletion is promised in the help center rather than in the contract. Insightly's article on managing a subscription states that data is retained for two weeks after cancellation as a grace period and then deleted. It also notes that the self-service cancel option deletes the account and all of its data at once, with no way to recover it.

Insightly's documents conflict on what happens to data when a subscription ends. The Terms of Service allow Insightly to delete a customer's data within 24 hours of expiration or termination and offer an export only on request in the ten days before the termination date, while the help center promises a two-week grace period after cancellation during which an administrator can reinstate the account. A customer cannot tell which rule applies, and Insightly should state a single retention period in both documents; until it does, agents should export their data before canceling.

A help-center notice about the Unbounce merger states that customer data is shared across the Insightly and Unbounce brands for purposes including personalized recommendations, cross-selling, and joint marketing, with an opt-out available by email. The notice does not specify whether customer data refers to Insightly's own account holders or to the contacts and records agents store. The Terms promise that Insightly will not access CRM data except to provide the service. Read together with the Privacy Policy, which leaves the data customers store outside its scope, the notice appears to cover account holders only, but Insightly should state that plainly in the notice itself.

How the privacy policy score was calculated Starts at 7, then:

  • +0.5Stating that customer data is private and not used to train AI models
  • +1Insightly handling that data only as a service provider
  • +1Committing to deleting the data after an account is canceled

7 + 2.5 = 9.5.

Conclusion

Insightly is a generic business CRM rather than a real estate system, and it suits no type of real estate buyer well: agents, teams, and offices would have to adapt it to their work at their own expense and would still lack some of the everyday tools a real estate CRM provides.

It does well at the core business of selling. Most users find it quick to learn; it handles shared work and supervision across a team effectively; the subscription is moderately priced, and its written terms are fair regarding trials, month-to-month billing, support, and the privacy of the records customers store. What holds it back is the distance between that general design and real estate work, and the gap between the company's written promises and what customers describe in practice, where slow or absent help, billing trouble, and lingering problems come up again and again, more so since the 2024 merger.

Two red flags deserve attention. Support is generous on paper but, according to many independent accounts, slow to respond in practice. And a customer's data can disappear quickly when a subscription ends because the company's own documents set different rules for how long it is kept; anyone leaving should export everything first.

As a purchase, the subscription is reasonable for what it does, but the paid setup and support a real estate buyer would likely need to adapt it would raise the first-year cost several times over, and the result would still be a general tool. Insightly is a sensible choice for a small business outside real estate; agents and brokerages will get more for their money from a CRM built for their work.

Alternatives

  • RealtyJuggler: A real estate CRM for new and established individual agents, agents with an assistant, and real estate offices, sold as a single plan at $179 per year. It costs less than a third of Insightly's typical plan and ranks at or near the top in every category, with real estate tools that require no adaptation.
  • Wise Agent: A real estate CRM priced at $499 per year that suits new agents, solo agents, agents with an assistant, and high-volume teams. It costs less than Insightly and scores far higher on features and reviews, giving agents ready-made real estate tools and better-rated support.
  • Close: A general-purpose sales CRM with a typical plan priced at $1,188 per year. Like Insightly, it was not designed for real estate, but it earns stronger review and contract scores at about twice the price.
  • Pipedrive: A general-purpose sales pipeline CRM with a typical plan priced at $468 per year. It shares Insightly's lack of a real estate focus but costs less and receives better reviews, making it the cheaper of the two generic options.
  • Follow Up Boss: A real estate CRM priced at $696 per year, best suited for agents with an assistant and for high-volume teams. It brings the real estate focus Insightly lacks, with stronger features and better reviews, though its governance score is far lower.
  • Zoho CRM: A general business CRM priced at $276 per year for its standard plan. Like Insightly, it would need adapting for real estate, but it costs less than half as much and scores somewhat higher on features.
  • Brivity: A real estate platform for high-volume teams, also suitable for offices and franchises, priced at $3,588 per year for its typical plan. It provides the team-oriented real estate tools Insightly lacks and earns top marks for support, but at roughly six times the price.
  • HubSpot: A general business CRM whose typical Sales Hub Professional plan costs $1,080 per year. Like Insightly, it is not made for real estate, receives weak reviews, and costs nearly twice as much.
  • Monday: A general sales CRM whose typical Pro plan costs $1,008 per year. It shares Insightly's generic design and weak reviews but costs more and scores lower in support and governance.
  • Top Producer: A real estate CRM priced at $2,148 per year, suited to experienced solo agents, agents with an assistant, and high-volume teams. It offers the real estate focus Insightly lacks, but it scores lower on value and contract terms and costs more than three times as much.

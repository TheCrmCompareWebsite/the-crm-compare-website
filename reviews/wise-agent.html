Introduction

Wise Agent is a CRM built specifically for real estate. The company markets it to agents, teams, and brokerages alike. The product is built around a single flat-priced subscription that a solo agent and a handful of colleagues can share, with a separately quoted edition for brokerages with 15 or more agents. It aims to keep lead follow-up, client relationships, marketing, and closings all in one place. It also offers transaction management capabilities as well.

Brandon Wise, then a working Realtor, started the company in 2002 with his friend Mike Becker, a programmer, to build the system he wanted for his own business. More than twenty years later, the two still lead it from Fountain Hills, Arizona, with Wise as chief executive and Becker as chief technology officer. Wise Agent remains independent, selling one CRM and nothing else.

Its appeal is a low price paired with personal service. One plan includes the whole system. Membership runs month to month, and setup help, one-on-one coaching, and round-the-clock phone support come with every subscription instead of being sold as service tiers.

Fit

Wise Agent suits established solo agents best, followed closely by agents with an assistant, high-volume teams, and agents who are just starting.

An established solo agent gets nearly everything needed to work new leads and stay in touch with past clients and referral sources in one low-priced plan, and much of the routine follow-up runs on its own. For an agent who wants the most capability for the money, few systems come close.

An agent who hires an assistant can add that person to the same account at no extra cost, or give them a login of their own with limits on what they can see, export, or delete. Work is easy to hand off and track, so the agent can stay in the field while the assistant keeps follow-up, mailings, and closings moving.

A high-volume team can route new leads to the right person, automatically start follow-up, and see which sources and team members are producing results. The whole team can work from one modestly priced account, leaving more of the budget for buying leads. The main reason a large team might pause is that a busy shared database has fewer safeguards for tracing and undoing mistakes than larger team systems provide.

A new agent pays little, gets a guided start, personal coaching, and someone to call at any hour, and can begin reaching people in the first week with ready-made campaigns and marketing materials. As the business grows into a sphere of past clients and, later, an assistant, the same system grows with it.

For a real estate office, Wise Agent is workable but not the strongest choice. The low price and free training help a broker who constantly brings in new agents, and the broker can oversee agents' work while keeping each agent's contacts private. However, brokerage reporting and volume pricing are available only in the separately quoted edition, and the short trial means the office pays for every new agent from the first month, including those who leave soon after joining.

Two concerns apply to every buyer. Opinions are divided on how easy the system is to learn, and the contract favors Wise Agent, which retains fees already paid and may close an account at its discretion.

Features 8/10

Wise Agent offers a broad feature set for a CRM at its price.

Wise Agent covers the everyday work of a real estate agent unusually well for its price. The typical plan includes contact records, mass and drip email with tracking, more than 20 stock drip campaigns, printed letters and labels, texting, lead parsing and routing, automatic Zillow lead delivery, transaction checklists with contingency deadlines and client portals, built-in mortgage calculators, commission and goal tracking, and Google sync.

Automation and sharing are strengths. Lead rules, pipeline status changes, and checklist triggers automatically start drips, texts, alerts, and tasks, and a small team can share one database, calendar, and set of transactions, with permissions that keep each login to its own work.

The weak spots are infrastructure and a handful of specialist tools: there is no account-wide change log or documented way to undelete records; CMAs require the separate Cloud CMA service; call transcription is available only from third parties; phone features depend on a paid dialer; listing alerts require a Wise Agent website; and some team reports are reserved for Enterprise.

Several less common extras are included in the base plan: unlimited contacts, a lead-enhancement service with monthly credits, continuing-education tracking, an AI writing assistant, lead-capture landing pages built from home valuation, guide and open house templates, and a documented developer API with webhooks. Importing is generous in service but plain in software: support will format a member's contact file and set up email parsing for a new lead source at no charge, but the import tool matches each file to a fixed list of column headers and retains no saved layouts for a particular lead source.

Several features earn half credit because Wise Agent covers only part of what each feature calls for. Contacts and vendors live in separate lists, but prospects are not kept in a separate database. Email merge fields combine names and salutations, but custom fields are not documented as merge fields. Tasks include due dates, assignments, and reminders, but only calendar events can repeat. Drip and checklist timings run a set number of days from a start or due date, with no documented recalculation when that date moves. Completing a call prompts for the next contact date, but there are no automatic touch-cycle reminders. Gmail conversations can be viewed and saved on a contact record, but this is not a full two-way mail sync. Listing import works only for the agent's own listings from about 20 partner MLSs. Reports cover any date range but never compare periods side by side. Each contact's activity tracker records changes to that contact, but there is no account-wide change log. Outside collaboration with notes works only with other Wise Agent subscribers.

Other half credits relate to price or plan. Click-to-dial comes from the Built-in Power Dialer by WAVV, a separate monthly subscription billed through Wise Agent. Pricing is not published. The same add-on dials three lines at once, records calls, and drops prerecorded voicemails. Per-agent activity reports (covering calls, emails, texts, and logins) and white-label branding are exclusive to the Enterprise plan. Saved searches, listing alerts, and market reports come with Wise Agent's own IDX websites, which are priced per site. Texting itself requires a WiseText number at $11 a month with 1,000 credits, plus an $80 carrier registration. Because those are telephony and carrier fees, the texting features earn full credit.

Some features could not be scored because Wise Agent's documentation neither confirms nor rules them out, so they are left unverified rather than scored zero: calendar export, template export, client self-booking, text read status, group texts, period-over-period and ranked agent reports, showing and offer tracking, earnest money and conveyance fields, electronic lockboxes, undeleting records, restoring earlier versions, the number of stock letters, a seller net sheet, and a rent-versus-own calculator.

A few useful tools cost extra or are provided by partners. An AI texting bot that qualifies new leads costs about $1.50 per conversation, and WiseSocial ($20 a month, powered by Back At You Media) automatically posts new listings to social media. As of October 2026, Claude and ChatGPT can add contacts and notes to Wise Agent via Zapier's MCP connector, though Wise Agent does not run an MCP server of its own. E-signatures use a DocuSign integration, and contract forms use zipForm, both of which require separate accounts. A Do Not Contact setting blocks texting, email, and dialing for selected contacts.

This review compares single-user prices, but Wise Agent prices its CRM for small teams. A team of 10 stays on the same plan and adds a second batch of five members for $20 a month, bringing the total to $739 a year with the base plan billed annually. Positive

Support is by far the most consistent theme. Reviewers across every site describe quick, patient help from named staff at any hour. One recent reviewer wrote that "A real human got back to me in under 5 minutes on a Sunday" (Google Business Profile, Jun 2026), and others credit the free onboarding and training sessions with getting them started. Many link that support to ease of use: "The system is easy to use, and the customer support has honestly been the best part" (Google Business Profile, May 2026).

Value and breadth are other recurring strengths. Agents say Wise Agent does more than pricier CRMs and credit it with handling their marketing, contacts, and transactions in one place.

Negative

Opinions on ease of use are mixed. For every reviewer who finds the system simple, others describe a long learning curve, an interface that looks dated, templates that are hard to work with, tutorials that are hard to find, and a lack of a standalone mobile app. Some also note that landing pages, texting, and other extras cost more on top of the base price.

Billing complaints do not form a pattern. The most pointed recent complaint came from a Google Business Profile reviewer who was charged $80 while setting up texting. Wise Agent replied in detail, explaining that the fee goes to the carriers' registration body and that the line was approved once the registration was resubmitted. The only other refund complaint in the reviews we studied dates back to 2017.

Value 8/10

Wise Agent's typical plan, CRM, costs $499 per year, and its normalized feature score is 8.0, so each feature point costs about $62. Few real estate CRMs combine a feature score this high with a price this low, and one subscription covers up to five team members on a shared login. Agents who need the power dialer, AI texting bot, or separate logins will pay more, but the base plan alone delivers most of the value.

Terms of Service 7/10

Wise Agent offers a 14-day free trial, but its sign-up form requires credit card details before the account opens. The Terms of Service automatically start the paid subscription when the trial ends unless the agent cancels first. From then on, the service runs month to month with no contract, and paying annually is optional.

Leaving requires some planning. Cancellation must be requested by email. The Terms require "30 days' notice before the subsequent billing cycle, without proration," and state that "All fees paid to Wise Agent are non-refundable." As a result, an agent who decides late in a billing month pays for one more cycle, and an annual subscriber who stops early gets nothing back. Wise Agent can also "adjust the fees at any time" with 30 days' notice, though prices have held at $49 a month or $499 a year since at least early 2024 (Forbes Advisor). Disputes are handled under Arizona law.

The obligations run mostly one way. The agent must give a month's notice to leave and forfeits whatever has been paid, while the Terms allow Wise Agent to suspend or terminate an account in its sole discretion, "without prior notice, for any reason," to keep the fees already paid, and to immediately deactivate or delete the account and its files. An agent who pays for a year in advance therefore risks losing both the prepaid months and the database without warning, and Wise Agent's own documents describe these rules inconsistently, as explained in the privacy discussion below.

How the score was calculated Starts at 7, then:

  • +1The free trial
  • +0.5The shortest plan runs month to month
  • −0.5The trial requires a credit card
  • −1Wise Agent can end an account for any reason and keep the fees already paid

7 + 1.5 − 1.5 = 7.

Support 10/10

Support is Wise Agent's defining strength. Its support page promises free 24/7 help by phone, email, and live chat "for all members—no upsells, tiers, or hidden costs." The support number is listed on the contact page. The team is in-house in Fountain Hills, Arizona; nearly all of the company's people on LinkedIn are in the United States.

Getting started is free. Every new member, including trial users, can book a complimentary getting-started session to set up the account, connect lead sources, and import contacts. One-on-one training is unlimited. Members can also email their contact file to support for import, and support sets up email parsing for lead sources that are not yet supported.

Self-help is extensive: the help center has more than 500 articles, dozens of embedded tutorial videos, and live and on-demand classes. For email support, verification of members' sending domains and guidance on staying out of spam folders are provided.

Reviews back this up: fast, friendly support is the most common theme in the reviews we studied, with support complaints outnumbered six to one.

  • +1Phone support on all plans
  • +1Free onboarding
  • +1Free one-on-one training
  • +0.5Email deliverability help
  • +1Free data-import help

7 + 4.5 = 11.5, above the top of the scale, so 10.

Governance 8.5/10

Wise Agent's governance score is the average of its company score and its privacy policy score.

Company 9/10

Wise Agent is a long-established, founder-run software company with a clean public record.

Wise Agent, LLC is an Arizona company founded in 2002 by Brandon Wise, who remains CEO, and co-founder Mike Becker, its CTO (Our Company). It sells a single real estate CRM and has no brokerage, mortgage, or title business. We found no outside funding, acquisitions, lawsuits, or negative news. The wiseagent.com domain dates to 2004. LinkedIn lists 11 to 50 employees, and HousingWire named Wise Agent a 2025 Tech100 winner.

The company is easy to verify. Its address and phone number are listed on the website, its Google Business Profile has 307 reviews, and the BBB rates it A+ with no complaints, though it is not BBB-accredited. Employee reviews on Glassdoor are less positive, averaging 2.7 stars from 21 reviews.

  • +0.5Being more than 10 years old
  • +0.5Its Google Business Profile listing
  • +0.5A BBB grade of A+
  • +0.5Its domain being more than 10 years old

7 + 2 = 9.

Privacy Policy 8/10

Wise Agent's documents keep agents' client data out of resale and marketing and limit Wise Agent's own use of it to running the service, but they promise nothing about that data once an account ends.

The Privacy Notice states that "We do not sell, rent, or lease customer CRM data entered into our system" and that Wise Agent does not allow third parties to use that data for their own marketing. The User Agreement licenses an agent's content to Wise Agent "Solely for purposes of delivering the Service and administering the System" and only for the life of the subscription; beyond that, what Wise Agent may keep and use is aggregated data that identifies no one.

The notice's broader passages, which allow marketing to and sharing information about people who visit wiseagent.com, apply to Wise Agent's own subscribers and site visitors. They do not cover the leads an agent gathers through a website Wise Agent builds: those sites carry the agent's own privacy policy and terms, as on lavieupstate.com.

The weaker points concern the future. Wise Agent treats the information it collects as "a business asset" that could be transferred to a buyer, and "does not guarantee data availability following termination," stating only that it may delete customer data after a reasonable period. Agents are responsible for exporting their contacts before canceling.

The Privacy Notice is inconsistent about how changes are announced. Its section on changes first reserves the right to change the notice "at any time and without notice to you," effective as soon as it is posted. The very next paragraph then promises to notify customers by email, text message, or other notification of any material change that affects how their Personal Information is collected or used. Wise Agent should state plainly which applies, so agents know whether they will be told before their information is handled differently.

The Terms of Service and the User Agreement set different cancellation rules. The Terms require "30 days' notice before the subsequent billing cycle," while section 6(b) of the User Agreement states "Cancellations take effect at the end of the current billing period" and sets no notice period. An agent who cancels two weeks before renewal owes another month under one document and nothing more under the other, so the two should be brought into line.

The two documents also disagree on when Wise Agent may terminate an account. The Termination section of the Terms permits termination "for any reason" without prior notice, yet the same Terms, under Default of Certain Customer Obligations, and section 6(b) of the User Agreement allow Wise Agent to terminate only for insolvency or a breach, and then only upon written notice. Until the documents agree, an agent must assume the broader clause applies.

How the privacy policy score was calculated Starts at 7, then:

  • +0.5Proactive statements that client data is not sold or used by third parties for their own marketing
  • +0.5The User Agreement limits Wise Agent's use of that data to delivering the service, even though it does not explicitly state that Wise Agent acts only as a service provider

7 + 1 = 8.

Conclusion

Wise Agent is built specifically for real estate and serves new agents, established solo agents, and even real estate teams equally well. A real estate office can make it work, but a franchise needs more than it offers.

Its strengths are its broad feature set, affordable price, and top-notch, responsive technical support. The everyday toolkit goes well beyond what most rivals include at this price. Reviewers praise the staff who answer the phone quickly, and the founders who started the company still run it, with no competing business on the side. Its weaknesses are an awkward user interface that can be inconsistent and sometimes difficult to learn. The system looks older than newer rivals and takes time to learn.

There are no critical red flags. While fees are not refunded, it offers a free trial, and month-to-month pricing is available. There is no guarantee of access to data after closing an account, so make sure to export your data before canceling, not after. Its privacy terms keep agents' client data and yours private.

At $499 a year, Wise Agent is one of the best values in real estate software. For solo agents, small teams, and agents starting out, it is easy to recommend.

Alternatives

  • RealtyJuggler: RealtyJuggler is a real estate-specific CRM for individual agents and small teams, sold as a single plan for $179 per year, with onboarding, training and phone support included. It costs about a third as much as Wise Agent and scores higher on features, value, and reviews, making it a closer match for agents who prioritize the lowest price.
  • IXACT Contact: IXACT Contact is a real estate CRM from Elm Street Technology, built around staying in touch with past clients and referral sources, priced at $561 per year. It targets the same solo agents and assistants as Wise Agent and is similarly priced, but it scores lower in reviews, terms, and company, partly because its owner now sells a competing CRM.
  • Follow Up Boss: Follow Up Boss, owned by Zillow, is a lead-conversion CRM popular with teams, priced at $696 per year on its Grow plan, with a 14-day trial that requires no credit card. It is a strong choice for agents with an assistant and for high-volume teams, and it offers friendlier trial terms than Wise Agent, but its privacy terms and ownership result in a far lower governance score.
  • Referral Maker CRM: Referral Maker CRM from Buffini & Company is built around Buffini's referral-coaching system and costs $468 per year, slightly less than Wise Agent. Its feature set is far narrower, so it suits agents already following the Buffini method more than those seeking a complete CRM.
  • Realvolve: Realvolve is a workflow-focused real estate CRM. Its Professional plan costs $790 per year plus a setup fee. It suits agents with an assistant and busy teams that want detailed automated workflows, but its reviews and support are much weaker than Wise Agent's.
  • Brivity: Brivity is an all-in-one real estate platform from PLACE, with its Growth plan priced at $3,588 per year. It targets high-volume teams that want more in one system, but it costs about seven times as much as Wise Agent.
  • Real Geeks: Real Geeks, owned by Fidelity National Financial, pairs an IDX agent website with a CRM for $3,990 per year, billed annually, with a minimum term and no free trial. It suits high-volume teams seeking a lead-generating website and strong support, but its contract, privacy terms, and parent company (title-and-mortgage) make it a much weaker overall choice than Wise Agent.
  • Top Producer: Top Producer is one of the oldest real estate CRMs, now part of Constellation1. Its Pro plan costs $2,148 per user per year. It targets the same experienced solo agents, assistants, and teams as Wise Agent but costs more than four times as much and requires a 12-month initial term.
  • Market Leader: Market Leader, owned by Constellation Software alongside Top Producer, sells online leads with a CRM attached. Its Professional plan costs $2,268 a year. Its CRM serves established agents and agents with an assistant adequately, but it costs more than four times as much as Wise Agent, and its reviews sit at the bottom of the scale, with many complaints about the leads it sells.
  • CINC: CINC, also owned by Fidelity National Financial, combines paid online advertising, agent websites, and a CRM for high-volume teams, priced at $11,988 per year for one agent before any advertising budget. It suits teams that want a steady flow of purchased leads and strong support, but it costs about 24 times as much as Wise Agent, and its contract and privacy terms are far more one-sided.

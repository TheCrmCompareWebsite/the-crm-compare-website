Introduction

IXACT Contact is a real estate specific CRM with an optional integrated IDX website. It is sold mainly to individual agents in the United States and Canada whose business comes from past clients, their wider circle, and referrals. A team version serves agents who work with an assistant or run a team, and brokerages can license a branded edition for their offices.

The product grew out of Morris Marketing Group, a Toronto firm that has written marketing materials for real estate agents for many years. It went on sale in 2007 and has been on the market for nearly two decades. In 2021, Elm Street Technology, a real estate software company in Frisco, Texas, bought both businesses; its chief executive, Keith Beeman, now leads the company that owns IXACT.

Its appeal is that the marketing comes with the software. The company writes the content, the system prompts the agent on whom to contact and when, and staff set up each new account, so an agent can stay in regular touch with a large circle of past clients without writing the material or designing the routine.

Fit

IXACT Contact is built first for the experienced solo agent, then for agents who work with an assistant, and finally for high-volume teams.

An experienced solo agent who relies on repeat and referral business gets a system that handles much of the follow-up: it tells them who to contact each day, remembers the dates that matter, and sends marketing the company has already written. New leads arrive without retyping, and the price is modest for that much help. The open question is why the parent company has just launched a second CRM aimed at this same agent, leaving it unclear how much attention IXACT will get from here.

An agent who has hired help can add a second seat for a small additional cost, work from a shared database, and control what the assistant may see, export, or delete. The routine work an assistant usually takes over, from client mailings and keep-in-touch campaigns to assigned tasks, is ready to hand off without building anything first.

A team that buys leads and works them in volume gets shared contacts, new leads flowing in from major lead sources, and a follow-up plan that starts right away, plus control over what each member can see and change. It distributes new leads more simply than larger teams usually do, so teams with several agents may need to manage assignments by hand.

A new agent receives ready-made marketing and a system the company sets up for them, so there is little to configure before getting to work. It falls short for this agent on cost and early support: the yearly price is a real expense at the start of a career, the free trial is short and requires a credit card, and personal help is limited to the initial setup.

A brokerage can license a branded version that includes its own setup, training, and support plan, and brokers can oversee their agents while each agent sees only their own clients. It falls short because pricing must be negotiated and the short, card-based trial does little to spare an office from paying for recruits who leave soon after joining.

All of these customers should weigh the company as well as the software. For several years, customers have found it hard to reach, though recent experiences have improved, and the product's development has slowed.

Features 7/10

IXACT Contact is built on strong relationship-marketing tools but held back by gaps in phone, financial, and integration features.

The product is strongest where a referral-based agent spends most of the day. Contact records are detailed and track who referred whom. Keep-in-touch reminders and reusable activity plans drive the task list, and email marketing can be scheduled, personalized, and measured. Letters, labels, and envelopes print in batches or on a plan's schedule. Texting works two ways, and listings and buyers are tracked with key dates and stored documents. Teams share one database with real permission controls, and contacts and calendars sync with Google, Outlook, and phones.

It is weakest in telephone interactions, in automation that branches based on client actions, in connecting to external systems, and in money management beyond commissions. Email doesn't sync in both directions, and the vendor's documentation shows no call capture, no expense records, and no reports that rank agents or project sales from the pipeline.

Ready-made content is the standout. Every account includes a monthly e-newsletter written by the vendor, hundreds of prewritten letters, 22 drip campaigns (15 added in August 2025), birthday, anniversary, and holiday e-cards, and Just Listed and Just Sold flyers, and teams can share templates and plans. The Keep in Touch Coach paces calls by each contact's status and turns the day's contacts into a short stack of cards, a workflow few CRMs build in. The plan includes an agent website with professionally written pages and a blog, with no limit on the number of contacts.

Several features stop short. Adding MLS listings to the website requires the paid IDX Broker add-on, and saved-search alerts for clients come only with it. Households record a spouse or partner but have no fields for children or pets, so their birthdays are kept as yearly events on the contact rather than as part of the family record. Activity plans date tasks when they are assigned and don't update when a closing date changes. Leads are shared out by lead type or first-come, first-served claiming only. Spreadsheet imports are matched column by column on every upload, with no saved importer for a particular lead source; leads that arrive by email are read automatically instead. Commissions are calculated on each deal and can be reported for any date range, though not compared period against period. Restrictions apply to assistant work by contact, and the record history shows who entered each item but not who changed it.

Some capabilities come from outside the product. Video email requires a separate BombBomb subscription, two-way texting needs a paid texting number, automated social media posting is a paid add-on, and transaction paperwork is handled by separate services such as SkySlope or TrackMyFile. The Zapier connection can only create contacts and leads, and there is no published programming interface.

Positive

Ease of use is the most consistent praise, mentioned in more than fifty reviews, with only a handful of complaints. Agents also value the ready-made marketing content, which several credit with bringing in referrals, as well as the reminders about whom to contact and the price. A longtime customer summed up the product side: "The product is fantastic. Easy to use, covers all the bases" (Google Business Profile, 2026).

Opinions on support are divided rather than settled. Older reviews and a cluster of 2026 Google reviews thank individual support staff by name; a reviewer who helped in October called one representative "Super helpful and great customer service skills" (Google Business Profile, Oct 2026). The mobile app draws mixed comments, praised on Android for quick access and criticized elsewhere for being slow.

Negative

The complaints are louder and more recent. Dozens of reviewers, many of them customers for five to fifteen years, say phone support was replaced by email around 2023 and that emails and voicemails then went unanswered for days or weeks. A broker who had paid a year up front wrote that "NO ONE will respond to an email or phone call" (Software Advice, Aug 2025), and a longtime Canadian customer found "no way I can get a response by calling the support line" (Trustpilot, Feb 2025). The company's public replies are mostly thank-you notes or invitations to call support, and none show a complaint being resolved.

Problems that require staff to fix tend to linger. Reviewers describe agent websites and listing feeds that have been broken for weeks, with listings missing from their sites. Billing and cancellation draw a second cluster: trial users say they could cancel only by emailing support or filling out a questionnaire; others say their accounts were deactivated without warning; and one waited a month for a cancellation to be acknowledged. Smaller groups of reviewers mention a learning curve for the less common features and a dated look.

Terms of Service 5/10

Agents can try the full product free for 14 days. Still, the signup page asks for a credit card before the trial starts and says payments begin when it ends and "renew automatically until canceled," so an agent who decides against it needs to cancel before the fourteenth day.

After the trial, agents can pay month to month with no contract or pay a year in advance. The Terms of Service allow a subscriber to cancel at any time by email with no notice period. Still, several reviewers say their cancellation requests went unanswered for weeks, so the easy exit the terms describe is not always what customers experience.

The obligations run mostly one way. Every payment is non-refundable, so an agent who pays a year in advance and leaves after a month forfeits the remaining months. IXACT, on the other hand, may end the agreement "with or without cause" at its sole discretion, and nothing in the terms refunds prepaid fees when it does. It may also raise charges with 30 days' notice and amend the agreement. An agent who does not accept a material change within 30 days can no longer use the service.

The agreement is governed by Ontario law, with disputes reserved to the Ontario courts and customers giving up jury trials and class actions, so a US agent with a billing or data dispute would have to pursue it in Canada.

Agents who buy a web address through the website add-on should also review the domain clause. IXACT registers the domain on the agent's behalf and renews it annually. Registration expires if a renewal charge is not approved, and dropping the domain requires 45 days' written notice before its anniversary. The terms do not state that the agent may take the domain upon leaving, so an agent who plans to keep a web address should register it personally.

Support 9/10

IXACT Contact's written support policies are generous, but reviews show those policies are often not followed, which is why its support score sits so far above its review score. On paper, every new account gets a free Concierge Setup Service, where staff upload the agent's contacts and set up the newsletter, email header, lead capture, website, and phone sync, then remain available on an ongoing basis.

The support page lists three ways to get help: a phone line available from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern, an after-hours web form, and email. The concierge page goes further, promising unlimited phone, email, and chat support on weekdays, though no chat option appears on the support page. Self-help is substantial: a help guide of about 50,000 words and a resources page that links to a playlist of tutorial videos and recorded webinars.

Reviews since 2023 contradict the phone promise: customers say the support line sends them to email, which often goes unanswered. The vendor's own getting-started FAQ now directs questions to the support email address rather than the phone. Some recent customers report faster help, but the advertised phone support still can't be relied on. Former support staff on Glassdoor describe working in Toronto.

Training is limited to setup assistance for individual agents; we found no one-on-one training sessions. Brokerages in the Broker Program receive a custom plan for implementation, training, and support.

Company 4/10

IXACT Contact is an established product from a parent that has just launched a competing CRM, run by a company that tells customers little about itself.

IXACT Contact Solutions Inc. was founded in Toronto in 2007 and operates as a division of Morris Marketing Group, a real estate marketing firm. In July 2021, Elm Street Technology of Frisco, Texas, backed by the private equity firm Aquiline Capital Partners, acquired both companies (Aquiline). At the time, Allan Goldstein ran Morris Marketing and IXACT. Elm Street's leadership page lists Keith Beeman as chief executive, but IXACT's website lists no one in charge, and PitchBook lists about 20 employees.

Elm Street retired its earlier CRM, Elevate, and an IDX Broker guide published in May 2025 moved Elevate customers to IXACT Contact. That consolidation has now been reversed. On November 2, 2026, Elm Street announced Grove, a real estate CRM for agents whose business comes from their sphere and referrals, sold under its own brand at getgrove.com and offered free until December 31, 2026, then at $60 a month. It is pitched to the same agent with the same promise of keep-in-touch marketing, so Elm Street now sells two CRMs that compete with each other. Its newest investment is going into Grove, and it is hard to tell whether IXACT will continue to get the same level of attention.

IXACT's own development has slowed. The blog shifted from dozens of product posts a year to marketing articles. The help guide dates from 2019, and the iPhone app is no longer in the App Store. The Android app was updated in August 2026, but the latest product announcement, from August 2025, is more than a year old.

The company is hard to pin down physically. The About page says it is headquartered in Seattle, but the contact page lists only a private mailbox there, while its Google Business Profile lists Morris Marketing's offices in North York, Ontario. Elm Street's BBB profile, which lists IXACT Contact among its other names, carries a C- grade and is not accredited, while IXACT's own Canadian BBB profile is not rated. The ixactcontact.com domain dates from 2008, and the site's copyright is current.

How the score was calculated The score starts at 7. We added ½ for a company more than 10 years old, ½ for a Google Business Profile listing, and ½ for a domain more than 10 years old. We deducted ½ because the only address on the website is a mailbox, 1 because the parent company sells a competing CRM (Grove), 1 for a BBB grade of C- or worse (Elm Street Technology's US profile, which covers IXACT Contact, is graded C-; IXACT's Canadian profile is not rated, so C- is the only grade to average), 1 because the website names no one in management, and 1. After all, the latest company news or upgrade notice is more than a year old. 7 + 1½ − 4½ = 4.

Privacy Policy 9.5/10

IXACT Contact's contract treats the agent's client data as the agent's property, uses it only to provide the service, and deletes it upon request or after the account ends.

The strongest protection is in the Terms of Service, which state that everything an agent uploads remains the agent's exclusive property. IXACT may use that data, and allow others to use it, only as far as necessary to meet its obligations under the agreement. The same section adds that IXACT "will not use your User Content for any purpose other than providing, managing and administering the services" it provides. A vendor FAQ states that an agent's client data is private and will never be sold, rented, or shared, and the security page says the same.

The Privacy Policy is primarily intended for visitors to IXACT's website and its customers. It prohibits selling, trading, or disclosing to third parties any personal information derived from the use of IXACT's online services without the customer's consent, except where the law requires it. It also holds the companies that work on IXACT's behalf to the same standard. The agent websites IXACT hosts carry no privacy policy of their own, so the leads they capture rely on these same protections.

Because IXACT's parent now sells a competing CRM to the same agents, the policy's one opening toward related companies warrants close reading. The Privacy Policy permits IXACT to share personal information with its corporate affiliates, but only to carry out transactions the person has requested or to make IXACT's or its affiliates' business more responsive to that person's needs. An agent's own client list is subject to a narrower limit in the Terms. Read together, the documents give Elm Street no permission to use an agent's clients to market Grove or its other products.

When an account is canceled, the agent can no longer log in. The Cancellation & Data Deletion Request page states that data is retained for 18 months in case the agent returns, after which it is deleted automatically, or it can be deleted at cancellation if the agent requests it. The terms allow IXACT to retain that archive longer, case by case, so agents should export their contacts first and request deletion when they leave.

One clause in the Terms of Service contradicts how the service actually works. Section 16(a) states in capital letters that "THE WEBSITE APPLICATION DOES NOT USE ENCRYPTION SECURITY," yet the agent websites IXACT hosts redirect every visitor to an encrypted (https) connection, as IXACT's own site does. Because those websites collect the leads described above, the clause understates the protection IXACT provides and leaves agents unsure what to tell their clients. IXACT should align it with its current practice.

How the privacy policy score was calculated Starts at 7, then:

  • +1The company commits to using the agent's data only to provide the service
  • +0.5It states that client data is private, never sold, and never shared (but says nothing about AI training)
  • +1It deletes the data on request or after an 18-month holding period

7 + 2.5 = 9.5.

Conclusion

IXACT Contact is a real estate CRM built for solo agents as well as agents in teams. It works well for both referral and repeat business, as well as for incubating fresh leads. The feature set is solid, and customers generally regard the product as reasonably bug-free. The software serves those agents well; what gives them pause is the company behind it.

Agents find IXACT easy to learn. The vendor provides extensive assistance with data loading, configuration, and training. The cost is modest, especially for what it offers. Its terms of service and privacy policy treat an agent's client data as the agent's own and limit the company's use of it to running the service.

Two red flags stand out. For several years, customers have reported that the company is hard to reach. Although recent experiences have improved, the advertised support still can't be relied on. And the product's development has slowed just as its parent has launched a newer CRM for the same agents, so it is hard to know how much attention IXACT will get from here. This is particularly notable given that the parent company has just released a new competing CRM called Grove. It is likely that attention will shift from IXACT to Grove over time.

Taken as a whole, IXACT is good value for the agents it was built for: a referral-based agent, alone or with an assistant, gets a broad, largely self-running system for a modest yearly price, and a team gets a capable shared system at a modest price per seat. They should test how quickly support responds during the trial and watch what Elm Street does next with upgrades. New agents and brokerages can make it work, though it is not built around them, and franchises will be better served by products designed for their scale.

Alternatives

At $179 a year, it costs about a third as much as IXACT Contact and scores higher in every section, though it is not aimed at high-volume teams like IXACT.
  • Wise Agent: A real estate CRM priced at $499 per year that covers nearly everything IXACT Contact does, from new agents to high-volume teams. It costs slightly less and scores higher across features, reviews, value, terms of service, support, and governance.

  • Referral Maker CRM: Buffini & Company's CRM for agents who, like IXACT Contact's core customers, build their business on referrals, priced at $468 a year. It costs a little less and earns a slightly better review score, but its feature set is far thinner.
  • Realvolve: A real estate CRM priced at $790 per year, aimed at agents with an assistant and high-volume teams, two of the groups IXACT Contact also serves. It costs more and scores lower on features and in reviews, but its terms of service are friendlier to the agent.
  • Grove: The new CRM from Elm Street Technology, IXACT Contact's parent company, launched in November 2026 for sphere and referral agents at $600 per year. It has well under half of IXACT's features and no reviews yet, so it is mainly worth watching as a sign of where the parent company is heading.
  • Brivity: A real estate platform from PLACE built for high-volume teams. Its typical plan costs $3,588 a year and includes a second user. It costs more than six times as much as IXACT Contact for fewer features, though it scores higher on support and privacy.
  • Real Geeks: An agent-website and CRM platform owned by Fidelity National Financial and built for high-volume teams, priced at $3,990 per year. It scores better on reviews and support, but costs about seven times as much, and its privacy terms score far lower.
  • Top Producer: A long-established CRM in Constellation's real estate group, aimed at the same experienced solo agents, agents with an assistant, and high-volume teams as IXACT Contact. Its typical plan costs $2,148 a year, nearly four times as much, and it scores lower on reviews, value, and terms of service.
  • Market Leader: A real estate CRM owned by Constellation Software, which also owns Top Producer, aimed at agents with an assistant and high-volume teams, priced at $2,268 per year. It scores higher on governance but far lower on features, reviews, value, and terms of service.

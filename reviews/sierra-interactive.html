Introduction

Sierra Interactive is a real estate specific platform that pairs a search-optimized IDX website along with a CRM built to work with the leads those websites capture. The company can help build the website and manage advertising to generate leads.

It includes support for MLS listing feeds, listing alerts, lead routing, and a built-in dialer. The company also runs Google and Facebook ad campaigns for its clients. The entry package is for solo agents and small teams, while the larger packages and most of the marketing target growing teams and brokerages that want their website to be their main source of business.

The company traces its roots to 2003, when founder Ben Peskoe began building real estate websites in the California ski town of Mammoth Lakes. Sierra Interactive was formally founded in 2007 and adopted a subscription model with its own CRM in 2017. Based in Louisville, Kentucky, it joined Alpine Software Group, backed by the private equity firm Alpine Investors, in 2022, when Peter Maglathlin became chief executive. Its main appeal is a single system where an agent's branded, search-friendly website and the CRM share the same lead data, so the agent who follows up can see every visitor's searches and visits.

Fit

Sierra Interactive is best suited to high-volume teams and real estate offices.

A high-volume team gets a lead engine built around its own website. Search-friendly property and neighborhood pages attract buyers and sellers, and the CRM shows what each lead is browsing, routes new leads to the right team member, and automatically starts follow-up, so a team that already spends on advertising can run its website, ads, and follow-up through one vendor. An inside sales agent on a team put it: "Behavioral triggers and lead activities make it so much easier to identify opportunities" (Google Business Profile, 2026).

A brokerage can put the office and every agent on a single branded platform, with agent websites under the office's name, oversight of each agent's activity, and recruiting tools, while preventing agents from accessing one another's clients. Seat prices fall steeply as the office adds users, so the platform is far more affordable at office scale than its single-user price suggests.

An agent with an assistant can share one database, limit what the assistant sees, and bring in new prospects to work. It falls short of ideal because the system is built for working fresh web leads rather than for keeping in touch with past clients and the sphere, where most of this agent's business comes from. Much of the everyday communication toolkit costs extra in the entry package.

Any buyer should weigh the yearlong agreement with no free trial, customer reports of slow support, and privacy terms that offer little protection for client data.

Features 4.7/10

Sierra Interactive has a strong lead-capture and team foundation, with real gaps in follow-up, transaction, and print tools.

The platform is strongest at the intersection of the website and the CRM: listings flow directly from agents’ MLS feeds, registered visitors become leads with their browsing history attached, and teams get the permissions and oversight a shared database requires. However, much of the day-to-day communication toolkit is an upgrade to the entry-level package, and the CRM offers little support once a lead becomes a client.

Lead handling is the clear strength. The typical plan includes the IDX website, real-time listing alerts, lead-capture forms, open house registration, lead source tracking, claimable lead pools, direct ingestion of Zillow leads, and email drip campaigns with open and click tracking. Team controls are equally comprehensive: a shared database, record-level permissions, limits on what an assistant can see, and a broker view of every agent's activity. Hosted landing pages for sellers and single properties, automated market reports, an AI assistant, a recruiting module, and a public API with webhooks add further value.

Many rows receive only half credit because they rely on the Sierra Dialer, a $100-per-month upgrade for Starter: texting, group and picture messages, bulk texts, call lists, click-to-dial, automated action plans, and the mobile app itself. Advanced lead routing and the agent leaderboard are available only with the larger packages. Emails cannot be scheduled for later, recurring tasks are not documented, and calendar sync is one-way.

Several capabilities agents rely on after the first contact are missing. There is no transaction management beyond recording a closing, no expense tracking, no ready-made touch cycle or birthday reminders, and Google contact sync requires a paid third-party service. Email-to-lead parsing has been retired for new setups, and imports accept only CSV files, with no saved custom importers. The help center does not document any letter, eCard, flyer, or newsletter library, print features, or sales-pipeline reports, so those rows receive no credit.

Positive

Praise centers on the websites' search performance and the leads they generate. Agents who invest in local content credit organic search for a steady flow of buyers and sellers, while others value seeing what each lead is browsing and the automations built on that activity. A twenty-year agent said, "the SEO capabilities are incredible," and a broker with 26 years in the business called it "the best platform I have ever used" (both Google Business Profile, 2026).

Negative

The most common complaint is about support. Reviewers on Google, G2, and Trustpilot describe emails and tickets that go unanswered, slow or automated replies, and problems left for the customer to work around. A longtime user summed it up: "Customer service is non-existent" (Trustpilot, Dec 2025). Only two reviewers praise the support staff.

Several reviewers say the sales process promised more than the product or onboarding delivered: features shown in the demo proved limited or were extra, websites that took months to launch, and results that never materialized. Some of the same customers say they were held to their contract when they tried to leave, and one says a database export failed at the end of the contract, while fees that were never explained appeared on the bills.

Bug reports form the third pattern: unanswered calls, a call list that deleted itself, mass emails sent to clients without the agent's approval, and automation glitches, with almost no reports of fixes. The mobile apps divide opinion, with nearly as many reviewers calling them clunky or unreliable as praising them.

Terms of Service 2.5/10

Sierra Interactive's terms are held back by the lack of a free trial and a yearlong commitment that customers can’t easily leave, while the vendor retains broad rights to end the relationship.

There is no free trial; prospective customers book a demo and sign up from there. Both billing options include a 12-month agreement: paying monthly does not make the plan month-to-month; it only raises the rate and adds a setup fee (Realty Tech Hub). Neither the pricing page nor the Terms of Service explains what happens when that year ends, and no renewal term is published. A long-time customer on Reddit reported receiving two weeks' notice that month-to-month contracts were moving to annual terms at a higher price (Reddit, 2024).

The balance of power favors the vendor. Sierra may terminate or suspend an account "for any reason whatsoever" without prior notice, and paid subscription fees are non-refundable. As a result, a customer whose account is closed has no stated right to a refund. Sierra may also change its fees at the end of any billing cycle after giving notice. Reviewers who tried to leave early report being held to their contracts, which the agreement allows.

One point favors the customer: the agent retains ownership of the website's domain name, and Sierra manages only its DNS while the site is hosted (help center). Disputes are governed by Kentucky law.

Support 10/10

Every Sierra Interactive package includes phone support, onboarding with training, help moving an existing database, and help with email deliverability.

Phone support is available weekdays from 9 am to 6 pm Eastern, with an after-hours line for emergencies. Callers who reach no one are asked to leave a voicemail (support guide). The company describes its phone and email support as US-based (Sierra Interactive). The phone number does not appear on the contact page, which lists only an email address, and the pricing page lists only email support, so new customers may not realize phone support exists.

Onboarding lasts 30 days on Starter and 90 days on Growth. It pairs the customer with a project coordinator and a customer education specialist for scheduled training sessions. Sierra's team imports the customer's existing lead database during setup (implementation page). Support staff also verify and install the email authentication records that keep messages out of spam folders (help center)—a large help center, a video library, and live training sessions back this up.

These written commitments are far stronger than what many customers report. Slow or unanswered support requests are the most common complaint in the reviews, and one ten-year customer says support became more distant after the 2022 sale. Support appears generous on paper and during onboarding but is harder to obtain once an account is established.

Company 6/10

Sierra Interactive is an established, real estate-focused company that has been part of a private-equity-backed software group since 2022 and holds a weak BBB grade.

Founder Ben Peskoe began the business in Mammoth Lakes, California, and Sierra Interactive was founded in 2007 (Our History). It is now based in Louisville, Kentucky, and lists 51-200 employees on LinkedIn. In October 2022, it joined ASG, a software group backed by the private equity firm Alpine Investors, and Peter Maglathlin became chief executive (Inman). Development has continued since the sale, with new website designs released in 2026 and help articles updated through October 2026.

ASG owns other real estate software businesses, including Homebot, a homeowner-engagement service primarily used by mortgage loan officers, and NestReady. None of these is a competing CRM, and ASG does not itself make loans or sell title or insurance. In April 2026, Sierra announced an integration that brings Homebot's homeowner signals into the CRM, which agents can choose to connect (press release).

The company has a Google Business Profile, but its BBB profile has a D+ rating, with one unanswered complaint since the file opened in 2025, and it is not accredited. Its street address appears only in the Privacy Policy, not on the contact page.

Privacy Policy 6/10

Sierra Interactive's privacy policy contains little that puts agents' client data at risk, but it makes almost no commitments to protect it.

The vendor's Privacy Policy is primarily intended for visitors to Sierra's website and for agents who hold accounts. The agent websites that Sierra builds link to the agent's own privacy policy, so leads who register on those sites are covered by the agent's terms rather than by Sierra's.

The one passage that addresses CRM data concerns email. The policy states that some versions of the CRM are built on Context.IO technology and that its providers, DokDok and its parent Return Path, may access "your information" and use it under Return Path's privacy policy, with an opt-out available on Context.IO's website. Because that technology reads connected mailboxes, which hold the agent's conversations with clients, it would allow an outside company to use client correspondence for its own purposes.

For Google-connected email and calendars, the policy is more stringent: Sierra stores only conversations between the agent and contacts, does not add contacts to other mailing lists, and will delete that Google data on request. No similar promise covers the rest of the database. Neither the policy nor the Terms of Service says that client data stays private, that Sierra handles it only to provide the service, that agents can export it after leaving, or that it will be deleted when the account ends. Sierra may change the policy at any time, effective when posted.

The policy also allows Sierra to share information about its customers and former customers among its affiliates. In context, this covers agents' account information rather than their clients. Still, with Homebot, a service sold to loan officers among its sister companies, the policy does not rule out sharing client data within the group.

The Terms contradict themselves about ownership of agents' stores. The Content section says customers retain all rights to the content they post. Yet, the next paragraph states that content found on or through the Service is the property of Sierra Interactive LLC. The Termination section says ownership provisions survive the end of the agreement. Sierra should state plainly that leads, contacts, and notes belong to the agent.

The Context.IO paragraph names outside companies and an opt-out site but does not specify which CRM versions use the technology. Sierra should state whether any current version shares mailbox data with Context.IO's providers and remove the clause if none does.

Conclusion

Sierra Interactive is a real estate IDX Website and CRM platform built for high-volume teams and brokerages. For that audience, its website, lead capture, and team controls are genuine strengths.

However, the weaknesses are serious. The price is high for a single agent, the entry package charges extra for calling, texting, and the mobile app, and the tools for nurturing past clients and managing transactions are limited. There is no free trial, the agreement runs for a full year, and the terms give the vendor far more room to walk away than the customer. The written support policies are among the most generous available, yet reviews have noted slow support, overselling of the product, and unresolved bugs. Recent reviews have grown more critical.

The privacy terms pose no direct conflict of interest, but they offer little assurance regarding client data, and the weak BBB grade warrants caution. For a team or brokerage with an advertising budget and the time to build out its website, Sierra Interactive is worth considering, provided the promises made during the sale are put in writing. For individual agents, it is poor value.

Alternatives

  • RealtyJuggler: A simple, low-cost CRM that ranks near the top in every category at a small fraction of Sierra Interactive's price. It lacks Sierra's websites and lead generation and targets individual agents, assistants, and offices rather than lead-driven teams.
  • WiseAgent: An affordable all-in-one real estate CRM with stronger reviews, value, and terms, priced at about a seventh of Sierra Interactive's typical price. It serves new and solo agents as well as teams, whereas Sierra is built mainly for teams and offices.
  • iXactContact: A relationship-marketing CRM focused on staying in touch with past clients and the sphere, at a much lower price than Sierra Interactive. It suits established solo agents and agents with assistants who Sierra poorly serves.
  • FollowUpBoss: A lead-management CRM for teams that connects to many external lead sources and websites rather than providing its own. It costs far less and scores much higher on value and terms, though its governance score is low.
  • Realvolve: A workflow-driven CRM for agents, assistants, and teams, with far stronger terms and better value than Sierra Interactive. Its review score is also higher, though still modest.
  • Brivity: A team platform with CRM, websites, and lead tools, priced nearly the same as Sierra Interactive annually. It scores higher on features, reviews, and governance.
  • Lofty: An all-in-one team platform with IDX websites, lead generation, and a much broader feature set at a similar price. Its reviews and terms are also weak, but it offers more value than Sierra Interactive.
  • TopProducer: A long-established CRM with lead-nurturing capabilities that costs less than Sierra Interactive and shares its weak terms and reviews. It is aimed at experienced solo agents, agents with assistants, and teams.
  • MarketLeader: A lead-generation platform with a CRM for agents, assistants, and teams, priced lower than Sierra Interactive. It scores slightly lower on features and terms, with equally weak reviews.
  • CINC: A lead-generation platform for high-volume teams that work with paid leads, priced several times higher than Sierra Interactive. It offers equally strong support policies but weaker value and terms.

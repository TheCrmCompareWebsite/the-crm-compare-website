Introduction

Apptivo is a general-purpose business CRM, not one built for real estate agents. It is sold as part of a suite of online business applications for small and mid-sized companies across every industry. Its website lists real estate among the many industries it serves, not as a focus.

The company has sold Apptivo since 2009 and remains privately held. Its founder, Bastin Gerald, still serves as chief executive.

Apptivo's appeal is its breadth of features at a modest per-user price. Businesses can tailor the system to their way of working without hiring a programmer.

Fit

Apptivo is a general business CRM and is not an ideal choice for anyone buying a CRM for real estate work. It is sold to businesses of every kind and lacks the real estate tools agents rely on, such as tracking a sale from contract to closing, importing leads from real estate websites and portals, and ready-made marketing content for agents. An owner willing to invest the time could shape it for real estate work with custom fields, automation rules, and templates, but that requires considerable setup; some work would still have to happen in other software, and the result would remain a do-it-yourself system you would need to improve and maintain. Buyers should also consider that Apptivo’s development has slowed considerably over the last few years and that the company seems to be in maintenance mode.

Features 5.3/10

Apptivo is strongest in the tools every sales team needs. Leads, contacts, customers, and deals live in separate but linked apps; email and calendars sync both ways with Office 365 or Google Workspace, and automation rules create tasks, send emails and texts, and route new leads to the right person. Team controls are unusually deep for the price: an owner decides who can see which records and fields and who may export or delete; every change is recorded with the old and new values, and deleted records can be restored.

Its gaps are almost entirely on the real estate side. Apptivo's documentation provides no support for transactions, contingencies, showings, or listing deadlines; it offers no way to import MLS listings or portal leads, and it includes no letters, flyers, eCards, or real estate calculators. Apptivo also states that it cannot merge duplicate contacts and does not provide printed mailings, such as labels and bulk letters.

Much of the strength depends on the plan. Email sequences, texting and calling, and the Properties app are available only with Ultimate, while bulk email campaigns and lead routing start with Premium. As a result, agents on the cheaper plans get a noticeably thinner product.

The Properties app is Apptivo's closest approximation to a real estate module. It stores each property with photos, type, status, and sale or rental terms; links owners to deals from interested buyers or tenants; and tracks rental availability and rent per period through rental invoices. It is most useful for rental managers; for listing agents, it is a property database without showing, offer, or deadline tracking.

Some agent essentials are only partially available or must be built. Contacts have a standard birthday field, but closing anniversaries, children's birthdays, and keep-in-touch reminders would require custom date fields and automation rules. Text reminders for appointments, text alerts when a new lead arrives, and automatic task initiation or email sequences for a new lead are possible. Still, only if you build those automation rules manually, so the table gives them no credit. Reusable activity plans are limited to tasks and emails that fire when a deal changes stage. Email to Lead creates a lead from the sender's address without reading the lead's details from the message, and Google contact sync requires a Google Workspace account and leaves custom fields behind.

Beyond the table, the suite includes invoices, estimates, expenses, and a ledger; project management; a help desk with a customer portal; a partner portal where outside referral partners can submit leads; recruiting apps; management of several businesses from one login; and automatic recording of calls placed through the Contact Center. A published REST API and webhooks let a technical user connect other systems.

A few capabilities cost extra or require external accounts. Electronic signatures use prepaid credits (a $10 minimum at $0.25 per document). White labeling is reserved for the Enterprise plan. Texting and calling require the agent's own Vonage account and phone number, which the agent pays for separately.

Positive

Support is the most consistent strength. Dozens of reviewers describe fast chat support, screen-sharing sessions, and representatives they name, and one 2026 reviewer valued that the help comes from "a real person, not AI" (G2, Jan 2026).

Reviewers also find the system easy to use day to day, and many say it is flexible enough for a small firm to adapt it to its own way of working without outside help. Others value the ability to run several parts of their business from a single, affordable package.

Negative

Complaints cluster around the interface and reliability. Several recent reviewers describe the interface as dated and cluttered, and app-store reviews are the most critical, with Android and iPhone users reporting crashes, forced updates, and missing functions. Reports of unfixed bugs recur across five sites, including an October 2026 review on Apptivo's Google Business Profile that said reported problems were dismissed as working by design. A smaller group finds setup steep, though they are far outnumbered by reviewers who find the system easy.

Billing is the most serious complaint. Reviewers on Trustpilot and the Capterra family describe charges after canceling, refusals of refunds even on a pro-rata basis, and an account cut off after a failed card payment; a five-year customer wrote that "they held my database hostage the same way ransomware would" (Trustpilot, May 2026). Two buyers also say they were sold capabilities or implementation results that never arrived. A handful of reviewers, including an eight-year client in 2026, say support has become slow, but they are far outnumbered by those praising it.

Terms of Service 10/10

In Apptivo's terms, a free trial without a credit card, month-to-month plans, and the freedom to cancel anytime outweigh a crowded plan lineup.

New customers get a 14-day trial of the Ultimate plan without entering a credit card, and the trial does not roll into a paid subscription. Apptivo emails plan options before the trial ends (Pricing FAQ). Every plan can be paid month to month, and the vendor tells buyers they are "not bound by any contracts or commitments." Four paid plans split the features among them.

Leaving is straightforward. Under the Terms and Conditions, a monthly account that requests cancellation is not billed again and remains active until the end of the period already paid for. Money already paid is another matter: fees are non-refundable, and discounted annual plans are never refunded, so an agent who prepays for a year has committed to it. The refund complaints described in Reviews show customers running into this. Two reviewers say they were billed after canceling, too few to show that Apptivo ignores its own cancellation terms.

The balance of power tilts toward Apptivo in one respect. A customer who prepays for a discounted annual plan cannot recover any of it, even when leaving early. Yet Apptivo may close an account on its "reasonable belief" that the terms were broken, and that closure can include deleting all of the account's data. Nothing in the terms returns prepaid fees in that case.

A few clauses deserve attention. If a card payment is declined, Apptivo may make all unpaid fees immediately due and suspend the account. The agreement is written as a one-year term that renews automatically, though the same document allows a customer to end it at any time simply by not using the service. Apptivo may delete the data of an account left unused for 180 days, may change the terms by posting a new version, and sends disputes to individual arbitration in California.

Support 7.5/10

At Apptivo, every paid plan includes live help by phone, chat, and email, but onboarding and training cost extra, and the support team works largely from India.

The pricing page lists phone support 24 hours a day, five days a week, and chat and email support around the clock for the Lite, Premium, and Ultimate plans. The support line is listed on the Contact page. Self-help is extensive: the Answers knowledge base contains several thousand articles, along with a user manual, webinars, and video tutorials.

Getting started is a paid service. Apptivo offers onboarding training at hourly rates: a 60-minute Quick Start session starts at $125, additional remote training sessions are $125 each, and on-site days cost $850. Full implementation and data migration are quoted individually (Services).

Support is largely delivered from outside North America. Apptivo's Privacy Policy explains that phone and chat support may come "from one of our global locations outside your country of origin," and reviewers often name India-based representatives, with a few noting language barriers.

Company 8/10

Apptivo Inc. is a privately held, founder-led software company that launched its suite of business apps in 2009.

According to the company's Leadership page, Founder and CEO Bastin Gerald, who previously built banking software at CashEdge and supply-chain applications at Oracle, leads the company. No outside investors or ownership changes are on record (PitchBook). The company lists its headquarters in Plano, Texas, with offices in Chennai and Madurai, India. Its BBB profile and Google Business Profile still list it in Fremont, California, and a second Google listing covers the Madurai office. Apptivo sells no competing CRM and has no brokerage, mortgage, or title business that could create a conflict of interest.

Product news has slowed. Apptivo's product-update section has not posted since December 2024, though both mobile apps received maintenance updates in late October 2026 (Google Play). Apptivo holds an A+ BBB rating with no complaints, though it is not accredited, and its domain dates back to 2008. Employees rate it 3.9 out of 5 across 100 Glassdoor reviews, with 89% approving of the CEO. Those reviews don't echo a single 2025 Reddit comment claiming staff went unpaid. It is, however, a general business CRM rather than one built for real estate.

Privacy Policy 10/10

Under Apptivo's privacy commitments, customers own their data; Apptivo says it shares no client data with third parties; and its data processing addendum limits its handling of that data to the customer's instructions.

The Privacy Policy, last updated in July 2026, states that "Apptivo does not provide any of our client data to third parties" and that clients own their data, which Apptivo does not intend to use. Customers may import contacts, which Apptivo may use only to provide the requested service. The policy also prohibits using customer data for advertising.

The Data Processing Addendum, which forms part of Apptivo's terms of use, designates Apptivo as a "Contracted Processor" that processes the customer's personal data only on the customer's documented instructions. The Terms and Conditions state the same: Apptivo will not use content stored in an account for commercial or marketing purposes without permission, and will access it only when necessary to provide the service.

Leaving is also well provided for. Apptivo's Data Security page states that client data "can be deleted on demand at any time" via a self-delete feature, with business data cleared from backups shortly afterward. The addendum also allows a former customer to request a complete copy of its data in writing after the service ends.

Much of the policy's broader language, about sharing information with affiliated companies and integration partners and sending newsletters and offers, describes how Apptivo treats the account holder's own details rather than the contacts an agent stores. Leads that arrive through Apptivo's web forms land in the agent's account, where the client-data commitments apply.

The addendum's scope is unclear. It is titled a European data protection addendum, yet it states that it forms part of the terms of use and defines the data protection laws it follows to include those of any other country where they apply. Apptivo should state plainly whether its processor, return, and deletion commitments cover every customer.

The documents also disagree on how quickly data is deleted. The Data Security page states that business data is cleared from rolling backups shortly after an account is deleted. At the same time, the addendum sets an outer limit of six years after the service ends. Apptivo should provide a single deletion period that applies to all customers.

  • +1A former customer can have a complete copy of its data returned after the service ends
  • +0.5The policy states that client data is private, not shared, and not sold (three of the four protections)

7 + 3.5 = 10.5, above the top of the scale, so 10.

Conclusion

Apptivo is a general business CRM, not a real estate product, so it is a poor fit for every kind of real estate buyer, from a new agent to a franchise: whoever chooses it has to design their own real estate processes and keep part of the work in other software.

It offers what you’d expect from a well-run small-business suite: straightforward month-to-month terms and a free trial with no credit card required. The company is an established, founder-led company. Every plan includes live help, and most customers find the product easy to use and well supported. Its documents clearly state that customers own their data and can have it deleted or returned. On the downside, it lacks the real estate depth agents need; many users find the interface dated, and a consistent minority report billing disputes after canceling and unresolved issues.

The clearest warning sign is the lack of momentum. Product announcements stopped at the end of 2024, and recent releases have focused on maintenance, giving little reason to expect the product to evolve to meet agents’ needs.

Judged on price, Apptivo is good value, but for most agents, much of that value lies in business tools they will not use. It makes sense mainly for someone who runs a broader business alongside real estate and is prepared to build a real estate workflow manually.

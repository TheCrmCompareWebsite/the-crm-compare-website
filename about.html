How we score

Each CRM gets a score out of 10 in six categories. The final score is the average of the six, rounded to one decimal place.

Features

How the score is calculated Points on a checklist of 119 real estate CRM features:

  • Full feature1 point
  • Partial feature0.5 points
  • Missing feature0 points
  • Niche groupup to 7 points
  • Totalpoints earned of 119

Points earned ÷ 119 × 10 = score.

Reviews

How the score is calculated Starts at 7, then, for patterns found across customer reviews on G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, the BBB, Google and other sites:

  • +Repeated praise, such as fast, knowledgeable support or ease of use, or mostly four- and five-star ratings
  • −Repeated complaints, such as billing and cancellation problems or bugs that go unfixed

7 + points added − points taken off = score, kept between 1 and 10.

Value

How the score is calculated Cost per feature point, on a log scale:

  • Typical planits price a year
  • Feature scorethe Features score
  • Cost per pointprice a year ÷ feature score
  • Value formula17 − 5 × log₁₀(cost per point)

The result, rounded and kept between 1 and 10, is the score. The scale is logarithmic because real estate CRM prices run from under $200 to about $15,000 a year.

Terms of Service

How the score is calculated Starts at 7, then, for the contract terms:

  • +A free trial with no credit card, cancelling at any time, month-to-month plans
  • −No free trial, unpublished or complex pricing, long terms or notice periods, automatic renewals, price increases

7 + points added − points taken off = score, kept between 1 and 10.

Support

How the score is calculated Starts at 7, then, for the help a customer gets:

  • +Phone support, onboarding, one-on-one training, free help importing data, email deliverability help
  • −A hard-to-find support phone number, support based outside the USA and Canada

7 + points added − points taken off = score, kept between 1 and 10.

Governance

How the score is calculated The average of a company score and a privacy policy score. Each starts at 7, then:

  • +Company: older than 10 years, a Google Business Profile listing, a good BBB grade, a domain older than 10 years
  • −Company: a recent change of owner, selling a competing CRM, other conflicts of interest, a poor BBB grade
  • +Privacy policy: client data handled only as a processor, deleted on request and returned when the account ends
  • −Privacy policy: client data sold, shared with partners or kept after the account ends

(Company score + privacy policy score) ÷ 2 = score, each kept between 1 and 10.
